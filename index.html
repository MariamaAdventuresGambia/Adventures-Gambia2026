About me

It started with volunteering, not with tourism.

My name is Mariama Sanneh. I'm Gambian, I'm 32, and my love for nature goes back to when I was a teenager and started volunteering — with the Gambian Department of Parks and Wildlife Management, with the West African Bird Study Association, which is BirdLife International's partner here, and later with WWF and the East Atlantic Flyway Youth Forum.

I spent those years counting birds and learning the reserves, and somewhere along the way I promised myself that when I grew up I would do this properly, as a profession. So I went and trained for it: an eighteen-month programme at the National School of Forestry at Kafuta, finished in 2021, and field training since then with Dutch and British specialists — on the dragonflies of Kiang West, and on the migratory birds that follow the Atlantic flyway down to us each winter.

Birding gave me things I did not expect. It taught me patience, and it is still what quiets my mind at the end of a difficult week. Most of all it made me a listener — out here you almost always hear a bird long before you see it, and knowing the calls is what turns a walk in the bush into a good morning's birding.

"I speak five Gambian languages. In the villages we pass through, you are not a stranger — and neither am I."
